Best Terrarium Setup for Crested Geckos

By ScaleAndTail | Updated August 2026

← Back to Home

Crested geckos (Correlophus ciliatus) have become one of the most popular pet reptiles in the world, and it is easy to see why. They are relatively small, docile, visually striking, and famously low-maintenance compared to many other exotic species. Originally discovered on the South Pacific island of New Caledonia, these arboreal geckos thrive in cool, humid forest environments where they climb through dense vegetation and feed on fallen fruit and occasional insects. Replicating that environment in captivity is the key to a healthy, long-lived crested gecko, and the right terrarium setup makes all the difference. In this guide, we will walk through every component of a proper crested gecko enclosure, from tank dimensions and substrate to humidity, feeding, and the best products on the market.

Choosing the Right Enclosure

The single most important decision you will make for your crested gecko is the enclosure itself. Because these geckos are arboreal, they need vertical space far more than horizontal floor space. A common mistake among new keepers is using a low, wide tank meant for terrestrial species like leopard geckos or bearded dragons. That orientation wastes the vertical climbing room that crested geckos desperately need. For a single adult crested gecko, the minimum recommended enclosure size is 18 inches wide by 18 inches deep by 24 inches tall, though larger is always better. Juveniles can be kept in smaller enclosures temporarily, but they grow quickly and will need a full-size enclosure within their first year.

Screen cages are popular because they provide excellent ventilation, which helps prevent the stagnant, overly humid conditions that can lead to respiratory infections. Glass terrariums with screen tops also work well and do a better job of retaining humidity, which can be advantageous in dry climates. Temperature and Humidity

Crested geckos are uniquely tolerant among popular pet reptiles because they do not require high temperatures. In fact, they stress easily when temperatures exceed 80 degrees Fahrenheit for extended periods. The ideal temperature range is 72 to 78 degrees Fahrenheit during the day, with a slight nighttime drop to 65 to 72 degrees. This means that in most homes, no supplemental heating is required at all. If your home runs cold in winter, a low-wattage ceramic heat emitter on a thermostat can gently warm one corner of the enclosure. Always use a reliable thermostat to avoid overheating.

Humidity is where many new keepers stumble. Crested geckos need a humidity cycle that ranges from 50 percent during the dry part of the day up to 80 to 100 percent immediately after misting. The enclosure should dry out somewhat between mistings to prevent mold and bacterial growth. Misting once in the evening and once in the morning is typically sufficient, though you may need to adjust based on your local climate and season. A hygrometer is essential for monitoring humidity levels accurately.

Substrate Selection

The substrate you choose affects both humidity retention and the overall health of your gecko. Crested geckos spend most of their time climbing, but they do descend to the floor to hunt and explore. A substrate that holds moisture without becoming waterlogged is ideal. Coconut fiber substrates like Zoo Med Eco Earth are the gold standard because they are natural, mold-resistant, and hold humidity beautifully. Avoid cedar and pine shavings, as the aromatic oils are toxic to reptiles. Also avoid sand, which can cause impaction if accidentally ingested and does nothing for humidity.

Decor and Climbing Structure

Crested geckos are arboreal, so the interior of the enclosure needs to mimic a forest canopy. Provide plenty of vertical climbing opportunities using cork bark, bamboo, vines, and branches. Live plants elevate both the aesthetics and the functionality of the enclosure because they help maintain humidity and provide natural cover. Pothos, snake plants, and bromeliads are all excellent choices for a crested gecko terrarium. If you prefer artificial plants, make sure they are designed for reptile use and have no sharp edges. Your gecko will spend most of its time resting on branches and broad leaves, so provide multiple elevated hiding spots at different heights.

A humid hide is also recommended. This is a sheltered area with damp sphagnum moss that gives your gecko a high-humidity microclimate for shedding. Place it near the bottom of the enclosure where humidity is naturally highest.

Feeding and Diet

In the wild, crested geckos eat a mix of fruit and insects. In captivity, the most convenient and nutritionally complete option is a commercial crested gecko diet. Pangea and Repashy are the two most respected brands, and both offer complete meal replacement powders that you simply mix with water. These diets contain all the protein, vitamins, and minerals your gecko needs. Offering a small portion of diet three times per week is sufficient for most adults. Juveniles should be fed more frequently to support growth.

Many keepers also offer live insects as an occasional treat. Crickets and dubia roaches are ideal. If you use live insects, dust them with a calcium and vitamin D3 supplement before feeding. Repashy Calcium Plus is a well-regarded all-in-one supplement that simplifies the process by combining calcium, vitamins, and trace minerals in one powder.

Water and Misting

Crested geckos typically will not drink from a standing water bowl. They lick water droplets off leaves and glass after misting, which is why regular misting is so important. Some keepers use manual spray bottles, while others invest in automatic misting systems for convenience. If you use tap water, treat it with a water conditioner like Zoo Med ReptiSafe to remove chlorine and chloramines that can harm your gecko over time. A shallow water dish should still be provided as a backup, though your gecko may rarely use it.

Final Checklist for Your Crested Gecko Terrarium

  • Enclosure: minimum 18 x 18 x 24 inches, taller rather than wider
  • Substrate: coconut fiber or a bioactive soil mix
  • Temperature: 72 to 78 degrees Fahrenheit, no heat lamp needed in most homes
  • Humidity: 50 to 80 percent with a drying period between mistings
  • Climbing decor: cork bark, bamboo, vines, and live or artificial plants
  • Diet: Pangea or Repashy complete diet, three times per week for adults
  • Supplements: calcium and D3 if feeding live insects
  • Water: mist twice daily, use a water conditioner with tap water
  • Hygrometer and thermometer for accurate monitoring

With the right setup, crested geckos are one of the most rewarding reptile species to keep. They are active, personable, and relatively forgiving of minor care errors. By investing in a proper vertical enclosure, maintaining a healthy humidity cycle, and feeding a complete commercial diet, you will set your gecko up for a long and healthy life. These geckos can live 15 to 20 years in captivity, so your terrarium is a long-term investment in your pet's well-being.
