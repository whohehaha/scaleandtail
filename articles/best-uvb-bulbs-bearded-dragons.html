Best UVB Bulbs for Bearded Dragons

By ScaleAndTail | Updated August 2026

← Back to Home

Bearded dragons (Pogona vitticeps) are native to the arid interior of Australia, where they bask under intense sunlight for hours each day. They receive enormous amounts of ultraviolet B radiation from the sun, which their bodies use to synthesize vitamin D3 in the skin. That vitamin D3 allows them to absorb and metabolize calcium from their diet. Without adequate UVB exposure, bearded dragons develop metabolic bone disease, a debilitating and often fatal condition caused by calcium deficiency. Providing proper UVB lighting is the single most critical element of their captive care. In this guide, we will compare the best UVB bulbs and help you choose the right setup for your dragon.

Why UVB Is Non-Negotiable for Bearded Dragons

Metabolic bone disease, or MBD, is the most common health problem seen in captive bearded dragons. It causes softening of the jaw, limbs, and spine, leading to deformities, fractures, tremors, and death if left untreated. The root cause is almost always insufficient UVB lighting combined with inadequate dietary calcium. Even if you supplement with calcium powder and D3, nothing replaces the natural UVB pathway that allows a dragon to self-regulate its D3 production. UVB also plays a role in appetite and activity — dragons kept under inadequate lighting often become lethargic and lose interest in food.

T5 Linear vs Compact Fluorescent: Which Is Better?

UVB bulbs for reptiles come in two formats: linear T5 tubes and compact fluorescent spiral bulbs. Both produce UVB, but they differ in output and coverage. T5 linear tubes are the industry standard for bearded dragons because they produce a wide, even band of UVB that covers the entire basking area. They are also more powerful, meaning UVB penetrates further from the bulb. A T5 high-output bulb like the Zoo Med ReptiSun 10.0 can provide adequate UVB at distances of 12 to 18 inches when mounted inside a reflective fixture.

Compact fluorescent bulbs are smaller and cheaper. They work in standard lamp fixtures, which makes them appealing for smaller enclosures. However, they produce a narrower beam of UVB that covers a much smaller area. The UVB output also drops off more rapidly with distance. For a bearded dragon, a compact bulb is only suitable for a small enclosure or a juvenile setup. For any adult dragon in a standard 40-gallon or larger enclosure, a T5 linear tube is strongly recommended.

Understanding UVB Output: What Does 10.0 Mean?

Reptile UVB bulbs are labeled with a number that indicates the percentage of UVB in their output. The 5.0 bulbs produce approximately 5 percent UVB and are designed for tropical and forest species that receive filtered sunlight through canopy cover. The 10.0 bulbs produce approximately 10 percent UVB and are designed for desert species like bearded dragons that live under intense, direct sun. For bearded dragons, you should always choose a 10.0 bulb. Using a 5.0 bulb will not provide sufficient UVB and puts your dragon at risk of metabolic bone disease.

It is also important to understand that UVB output degrades over time. Even though a bulb may still produce visible light after a year of use, its UVB output drops significantly. Most manufacturers recommend replacing T5 bulbs every 12 months and compact fluorescent bulbs every 6 months. Mark the installation date on your calendar so you do not forget. This is the most common reason dragons develop MBD despite having a UVB bulb in place.

Fixture and Placement Recommendations

The fixture you use matters as much as the bulb. A T5 high-output bulb requires a compatible T5 HO fixture with a built-in reflector. The reflector directs UVB downward into the enclosure rather than letting it scatter in all directions. Without a reflector, you lose a significant portion of the bulb's effective output. The Zoo Med ReptiSun T5 HO Terrarium Hood is a popular fixture that includes a built-in reflector and is available in lengths that match standard enclosure widths.

Placement is equally important. The UVB bulb should span the full length of the basking area so your dragon receives UVB while it thermoregulates. Mount the fixture inside the enclosure if possible, or on top of a screen lid if you are using a glass tank. Be aware that screen lids can block up to 30 to 40 percent of UVB output, so mounting inside the enclosure is always preferable. The distance from the bulb to the basking spot should be 8 to 12 inches for a T5 HO bulb and 6 to 8 inches for a compact fluorescent.

Basking Lights and Heat: The Other Half of Lighting

UVB and heat are two separate lighting needs. A UVB bulb does not produce significant heat, so you will also need a basking bulb to create a warm basking spot of 95 to 110 degrees Fahrenheit. A standard halogen or incandescent basking bulb works well for this purpose. Position the basking bulb adjacent to the UVB bulb so your dragon gets both heat and UVB in the same area. This mimics the natural condition where sunlight provides both warmth and ultraviolet radiation simultaneously.

At night, all lights should be turned off. Bearded dragons need a dark, cooler environment at night for proper rest. If your home drops below 60 degrees Fahrenheit at night, a ceramic heat emitter can provide warmth without visible light. Always use a thermostat to prevent overheating.

Diet and Supplementation: Completing the Picture

Even with perfect UVB lighting, your bearded dragon needs dietary calcium to build strong bones. Dust all feeder insects with a calcium and vitamin D3 supplement like Repashy Calcium Plus before feeding. For juveniles, dust at every feeding. For adults, dusting every other feeding is sufficient. Offer a variety of dark, leafy greens daily. Collard greens, mustard greens, turnip greens, and dandelion greens are all excellent choices. Avoid lettuce, which is mostly water and offers little nutritional value. As a general rule, a juvenile bearded dragon's diet should be approximately 70 percent insects and 30 percent greens, while an adult should flip to approximately 70 percent greens and 30 percent insects.

Summary: The Best UVB Setup for Bearded Dragons

  • Choose a T5 HO 10.0 linear bulb for any adult dragon enclosure
  • Compact fluorescent 10.0 bulbs work for juveniles or small temporary enclosures
  • Use a fixture with a built-in reflector for maximum UVB delivery
  • Mount the bulb 8 to 12 inches from the basking spot for T5, 6 to 8 inches for compact
  • Replace T5 bulbs every 12 months and compact bulbs every 6 months
  • Pair UVB with a separate basking bulb for heat, positioned adjacent to the UVB bulb
  • Use a thermostat to regulate basking temperatures safely
  • Dust feeder insects with calcium and D3 supplement
  • Provide daily dark leafy greens in addition to insects

Proper UVB lighting is the foundation of bearded dragon health. No other aspect of their care is as consequential for long-term well-being. By investing in a quality T5 linear bulb, a reflector fixture, and a consistent replacement schedule, you are giving your dragon the best chance at a long, healthy, and active life. Metabolic bone disease is entirely preventable with correct lighting, and there is no excuse for cutting corners when it comes to UVB.
